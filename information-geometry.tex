One could sum up this whole document in a sentence:
\begin{summary}
The matrix that drove the training gradient, the second-order shape of the KL-divergence, and
the one coordinate-invariant way to measure distance between distributions are all the same
object --- the Fisher information metric --- and the space of softmax distributions carries
not one but a dual \emph{pair} of flat connections, one straight in logits and one straight in
probabilities.
\end{summary}
This is the fourth of a set. \emph{Next-Token Prediction is Geometry} built the Euclidean
picture (inner products, the softmax Jacobian, the spring-law gradient); \emph{Training is a
Monte Carlo Estimate} said what the loss measures (cross-entropy, KL); \emph{The
Radon--Nikodym Theorem} said what the densities are. Each used a geometry on the space of
distributions without naming it. This note names it. The field is \emph{information geometry}
(Chentsov, Amari, Nagaoka).

\section{Two manifolds, and which one carries the geometry}

``The manifold'' has meant two different things, and they carry different structure:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item the \textbf{representation manifold} --- hidden states $h$ and token embeddings $e_w$
  as points in $\mathbb{R}^d$, the ``meaning manifold'' of the first note, a curved subset of
  Euclidean space;
  \item the \textbf{statistical manifold} --- the \emph{output distributions} themselves, the
  family $\{\, q = \operatorname{softmax}(z) \,\}$ living in the probability simplex, each point
  a complete next-token distribution.
\end{itemize}
The canonical geometry lives on the statistical manifold. The representation manifold inherits
a meaningful geometry only by \emph{pulling back} from it --- the last section. We do the
statistical manifold first, because that is where every answer is clean.

\section{The Fisher metric}

Parametrise a family of distributions by $\theta$ and write the log-likelihood
$\ell_\theta = \log q_\theta$. The \emph{score} is its gradient $\partial_i \ell_\theta$, and
the \textbf{Fisher information metric} is the covariance of the score:
\[
  g_{ij}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{q_\theta}\!\bigl[\partial_i \ell_\theta \,\partial_j \ell_\theta\bigr]
  = -\,\mathbb{E}_{q_\theta}\!\bigl[\partial_i \partial_j \ell_\theta\bigr].
\]
It is symmetric and positive semi-definite --- a Riemannian metric on the space of
distributions. Three facts turn ``a metric'' into ``\emph{the} metric,'' and the second is the
spine of this note.

\subsection*{It is the second-order shape of the KL-divergence}

Expand the divergence between two nearby distributions. The first-order term vanishes (the
divergence has a minimum of zero at coincidence), and the first surviving term is the Fisher
quadratic form:
\[
  D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_\theta \,\|\, q_{\theta+\delta})
  = \tfrac12\, \delta^{\top} g(\theta)\, \delta + O(\lVert\delta\rVert^3).
\]
The KL-divergence of the companion notes is not a metric globally --- asymmetric, no triangle
inequality --- but infinitesimally it \emph{is} one: $g$ is precisely the thing being squared.
Distance-between-distributions and the loss we descended are the same geometry at two scales.

\subsection*{For softmax it is exactly the Jacobian from the first note}

Write the softmax family as the exponential family it is. With logits $z$ as parameters,
\[
  \ell_z(w) = \log q_z(w) = z_w - A(z), \qquad A(z) = \log \textstyle\sum_k e^{z_k},
\]
where $A$ --- the log-partition function --- is the $\log\sum\exp$ we keep meeting. Its gradient
is the distribution, $\partial_i A = p_i$, so the score is $\partial_i \ell_z(w) = \delta_{iw} -
p_i$, and the Fisher metric is its covariance:
\[
  g_{ij}(z) = \mathbb{E}_w\!\bigl[(\delta_{iw}-p_i)(\delta_{jw}-p_j)\bigr]
  = p_i\,\delta_{ij} - p_i p_j
  = \frac{\partial p_i}{\partial z_j}.
\]
\begin{summary}
\[
  g(z) \;=\; \operatorname{diag}(p) - p\,p^{\top}
  \;=\; \nabla^2 A(z)
  \;=\; \frac{\partial\, p}{\partial\, z}
  \;=\; \nabla^2_{\!\delta}\, D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big|_{\delta=0}.
\]
The Fisher metric of the softmax family, the Hessian of $\log\sum\exp$, the softmax Jacobian
$p_i(\delta_{ij}-p_j)$ that drove the spring law, and the second-order shape of KL are one
matrix written four ways.
\end{summary}
The identity is forced, not coincidental. Softmax is the gradient of the log-partition,
$p = \nabla A$, so its Jacobian is the Hessian $\nabla^2 A$ --- and the Hessian of a
log-partition is by definition the covariance of the sufficient statistic, which is the Fisher
metric. The matrix the first note computed as ``the derivative of softmax'' and used to push
embeddings around was the metric tensor the entire time; training did differential geometry
without declaring it.

More than carried along, the metric is \emph{applied} on the backward pass. Differentiating a
bare softmax gives $\partial L/\partial z = g\,(\partial L/\partial p)$, and since the logits
$z$ are the natural coordinates $\theta$ and the probabilities $p$ the expectation coordinates
$\eta$ (next section), $g = \partial\eta/\partial\theta$ is the Jacobian \emph{between the dual
coordinate systems}. So the softmax backward pass \emph{is} the Fisher metric at work:
converting the gradient written in probability-coordinates into the gradient written in
logit-coordinates --- index lowering by $g$. The familiar fused simplification
$\partial L/\partial z = p - y$ is exactly that metric multiplication, carried out in closed
form.

\begin{insight}
One caveat keeps the identity honest, because it is the standard place to go wrong. This is
the Fisher metric of the \emph{output distribution} in \emph{logit} coordinates --- an
$n\times n$ matrix. It is \emph{not} the parameter-space Fisher of natural-gradient fame, the
(parameters $\times$ parameters) matrix $F_\theta = \mathbb{E}\!\bigl[\nabla_\theta \log q\;
\nabla_\theta \log q^{\top}\bigr]$. That one is this matrix \emph{pulled back} through the
network,
\[
  F_\theta = J_{\mathrm{net}}^{\top}\,\bigl(\operatorname{diag}(p) - p\,p^{\top}\bigr)\, J_{\mathrm{net}},
  \qquad J_{\mathrm{net}} = \partial z/\partial\theta,
\]
the Gauss--Newton / K-FAC factorisation, and the pullback of \S5. The bare softmax Jacobian is
the \emph{kernel} that gets sandwiched, not the sandwich.
\end{insight}

\begin{insight}
The metric is rank $n-1$, not $n$: adding a constant to every logit changes nothing, so
$g$ has the all-ones vector in its null space --- the gauge freedom of softmax. On the tangent
space of the simplex (perturbations whose entries sum to zero) it is strictly positive
definite. The manifold is the $(n-1)$-dimensional simplex interior, and $g$ is a genuine metric
there; the degenerate direction is only the redundancy in the coordinates.
\end{insight}

\subsection*{It is the only invariant metric (Chentsov)}

Chentsov's theorem: on the simplex, the Fisher metric is the \emph{unique} Riemannian metric,
up to an overall scale, invariant under sufficient statistics --- the maps that reparametrise
without discarding information. If you want distance between distributions not to depend on
arbitrary choices of coordinate, you do not get to choose: it is Fisher--Rao or nothing. This
is what licenses the first note's whole premise that ``geometry tracks meaning'' --- there is
exactly one invariant geometry for it to track.

\section{Dual coordinates: logits and probabilities are Legendre partners}

An exponential family comes with two natural coordinate systems and a duality between them.
The \emph{natural} (canonical) parameters are the logits $\theta = z$; the \emph{expectation}
parameters are the probabilities themselves, $\eta = \mathbb{E}_w[\text{one-hot}] = p$. They are
related by the gradient of the log-partition and its convex conjugate:
\[
  \eta = \nabla A(\theta) = \operatorname{softmax}(\theta),
  \qquad
  A^{*}(\eta) = \sup_\theta\bigl(\langle\theta,\eta\rangle - A(\theta)\bigr)
  = \sum_w p_w \log p_w = -H(p).
\]
The dual potential is the \emph{negative entropy} --- the same $H$ from the coding story. So
the Legendre pair underneath softmax is
\[
  A = \log\textstyle\sum\exp \quad\longleftrightarrow\quad A^{*} = -H,
\]
and the Fisher metric is the Hessian of \emph{either} potential, in its own coordinates:
$g = \nabla^2 A(\theta)$ in logit-coordinates, $g^{-1} = \nabla^2 A^{*}(\eta)$ in
probability-coordinates. The maximum-entropy characterisation of softmax (why $\nabla A =
\operatorname{softmax}$) and the metric are two readings of one convex duality.

\section{The connections: not one, but a dual pair}

Here the statistical manifold is richer than an ordinary Riemannian one. A metric alone gives
the Levi--Civita connection --- one notion of ``straight line'' and parallel transport. An
exponential family carries a whole one-parameter family of $\alpha$-connections, and two are
distinguished:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item the \textbf{exponential connection} ($e$-connection, $\alpha=+1$): its Christoffel
  symbols \emph{vanish in the logit coordinates} $\theta$. The family is $e$-flat, and straight
  lines \emph{in logit space} are its geodesics.
  \item the \textbf{mixture connection} ($m$-connection, $\alpha=-1$): its Christoffel symbols
  \emph{vanish in the probability coordinates} $\eta$. The family is $m$-flat, and straight
  lines \emph{in probability space} are its geodesics.
\end{itemize}
These two are \emph{dual} with respect to the Fisher metric --- $\partial_k g_{ij} =
\Gamma^{e}_{ki,j} + \Gamma^{m}_{kj,i}$ --- and the Levi--Civita connection is exactly their
average, the $\alpha=0$ case. The softmax family is therefore \textbf{dually flat}: it has two
distinct canonical notions of straightness, and the metric geodesic is neither, but their
mean.

\begin{insight}
Dual flatness is what being an exponential family \emph{buys} geometrically, and it has teeth.
In a dually flat manifold the KL-divergence is the \textbf{Bregman divergence} of the potential
--- $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|q)$ is the gap between $A^{*}$ at $p$ and its tangent plane at $q$ ---
and a \textbf{generalised Pythagorean theorem} holds:
\[
  D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\|R) = D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\|Q) + D_{\mathrm{KL}}(Q\|R)
\]
whenever the $m$-geodesic from $P$ to $Q$ meets the $e$-geodesic from $Q$ to $R$ at a
Fisher-orthogonal angle. That right angle is the engine under the EM algorithm, information
projection, and variational inference: their decompositions are literal orthogonal projections
in this metric. Parallel transport, too, splits in two --- $e$-transport carries logit-vectors,
$m$-transport carries probability-vectors, and they are adjoint under $g$.
\end{insight}

\section{The pullback: a metric on the representation manifold}

Now the $\mathbb{R}^d$ manifold of hidden states. It trivially inherits the \emph{Euclidean}
metric of the ambient space --- that is what the inner product $\langle h, e_w\rangle$ uses ---
but that metric is not canonical: it depends on the coordinates the network happened to learn
and respects no invariance. The meaningful metric arrives by \emph{pulling back} the Fisher
metric through the map that turns a hidden state into a distribution. With logits $z = Wh$ (the
unembedding), the pulled-back metric on hidden-state space is
\[
  \hat g(h) = W^{\top} \bigl(\operatorname{diag}(p) - p\,p^{\top}\bigr) W,
  \qquad p = \operatorname{softmax}(Wh),
\]
which is the parameter-space Fisher $F_\theta$ of \S2's caveat, restricted to the unembedding
layer --- the bare Jacobian $\operatorname{diag}(p) - p p^{\top}$ sandwiched by $W$. It has a
direct reading through the KL expansion of \S2:
\[
  D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(q(h) \,\|\, q(h+dh)\bigr) = \tfrac12\, dh^{\top}\hat g(h)\, dh + \cdots.
\]
Two hidden states are near in $\hat g$ exactly when they induce \emph{similar next-token
distributions}. This is the distributional hypothesis --- \emph{you shall know a word by the
company it keeps} --- promoted from slogan to Riemannian metric: semantic distance is
difference in predictive behaviour. And it is in general \emph{not} the Euclidean distance on
$h$; it is the Euclidean distance warped by how sharply the output reacts, the warp factor
being the Fisher metric at $p$.

\begin{insight}
This is the precise sense in which the first note's Euclidean picture was a chart, not the
territory. The gradient $\partial L/\partial h = \bar e - e_y$ it derived is the
\emph{Euclidean} gradient; the geometry-respecting update is the \emph{natural gradient}
$\hat g^{-1}\,\partial L/\partial h$, which moves along the manifold rather than across the
coordinates. Natural-gradient descent is ordinary gradient descent performed in the Fisher
metric --- doing the geometry the first note only gestured at.
\end{insight}

\section{Two honest caveats}

The elegance should not oversell itself.

\textbf{Flat ambient, curved slice.} The full simplex is dually flat, but the submanifold the
network actually sweeps out --- the image of $h \mapsto \operatorname{softmax}(Wh)$ --- is in
general a \emph{curved} submanifold of it. Its embedding curvature measures how far the model's
own family is from being an exponential family in its own right. ``Flat'' describes the ambient
statistical manifold, not the model's slice through it.

\textbf{Smoothness, and the density underneath.} All of this assumes a smooth manifold. The
first note already flagged that the ``meaning manifold'' is loose --- ReLU kinks, varying rank,
stratification --- so the apparatus is airtight on the statistical manifold and only as
rigorous as the map $h \mapsto q$ is nice on the representation side. And the Fisher metric is
defined through $\ell = \log q$ and its score $\partial \log q$ --- densities, which is to say
Radon--Nikodym derivatives. In the finite, strictly-positive softmax world those exist for
free and the metric is elementary; let the vocabulary become a continuum and whether the metric
exists at all becomes the same Radon--Nikodym question the third note ends on. The geometry,
like everything else in this set, rests on a foundation that is silent here and load-bearing
the moment the sum becomes an integral.
